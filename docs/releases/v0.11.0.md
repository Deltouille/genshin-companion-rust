Genshin Companion 0.11.0 keeps what HoYoLAB forgets: your Spiral Abyss, Imaginarium Theater and Traveler’s Diary go into a new Chronicle, for good. It can also remember your HoYoLAB cookie in your system’s keyring, so the Chronicle syncs on its own.

## Download

| System | Archive |
|---|---|
| Windows (x86_64) | `genshin-companion-0.11.0-windows-x86_64.zip` |
| macOS 11 or later, Apple Silicon | `genshin-companion-0.11.0-macos-apple-silicon.zip` |
| macOS 11 or later, Intel | `genshin-companion-0.11.0-macos-intel.zip` |
| Linux (x86_64, glibc 2.35 or later) | `genshin-companion-0.11.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` |

Unpack the archive and start the app: there is nothing to install. Your data stays where it was, so a newer version picks up your history, plan and settings. `SHA256SUMS.txt` holds the archives’ checksums.

The builds aren’t signed by a developer account, so the first launch asks for confirmation:

- **Windows:** if SmartScreen warns, choose *More info*, then *Run anyway*.
- **macOS:** macOS refuses to open it the first time. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose *Open Anyway*.
- **Linux:** the app needs fontconfig, freetype, libxkbcommon, Wayland, libX11, libxcb and the Vulkan loader, which desktop systems usually have.

## What’s new

**The Chronicle.** HoYoLAB shows your Spiral Abyss and Imaginarium Theater for the period under way and the one before, and your Traveler’s Diary for three months; then they’re gone. The HoYoLAB sync now keeps them, and *Chronicle* in the sidebar shows them:

- *Spiral Abyss*: each period’s stars, deepest chamber and battles, its records (most used, strongest strike…), and the teams that cleared each chamber, with the stars of every period kept on a chart;
- *Imaginarium Theater*: each period’s difficulty, furthest act, medals and Fantasia Flowers, its highlights, and each act with its cast (trial and support characters marked);
- *Traveler’s Diary*: each month’s primogems and Mora, where the primogems came from, day by day, and every reward one by one;
- HoYoLAB’s answers are kept whole, so a later version can show more of what is already kept;
- a period kept once it is over never changes; one under way is brought up to date at the next sync, so syncing once a month misses nothing;
- the sync’s preview says how many periods it keeps or updates, and nothing is written before you confirm.

**Remember the cookie.** Tick *Remember this cookie on this computer* in the sync’s dialog, and the app keeps your login (`ltuid_v2`, `ltoken_v2`, `ltmid_v2`; never `cookie_token_v2`) in your system’s keyring, nowhere else. The Chronicle then syncs on its own while the app is open, when it opens and every 6 hours:

- only the Chronicle, never your characters (those still wait for a sync you confirm), and only into the account that has that UID;
- a backup first when something changes, at most one an hour;
- Settings → HoYoLAB shows the cookie kept, syncs now, or forgets it;
- if HoYoLAB no longer accepts the cookie, it is forgotten and a message asks for a new one; if HoYoLAB asks for a captcha, the syncs on their own stop until the app opens again.

**Smaller changes:**

- `wishctl hoyolab-check` also says whether the Spiral Abyss, Imaginarium Theater and Traveler’s Diary answers read as the app reads them.

## Good to know

- Your database gains the Chronicle’s table. The app takes a backup before changing it, and once 0.11.0 has opened your data, 0.10.0 refuses to open it rather than risk it.
- Snapshots (and the mirror file) now carry the Chronicle: they are version 3. 0.11.0 still reads older ones; 0.10.0 refuses one written by 0.11.0 and says why.
- An account with a Chronicle can’t be removed, like one with wishes: nothing else holds that data.
- The keyring encrypts the cookie on disk, but any program running as you can ask for it. On Linux it needs a Secret Service (GNOME Keyring, KWallet, KeePassXC); without one, the cookie isn’t kept. On macOS, since the builds aren’t signed, the first launch of each new version asks before reading it: choose *Always Allow*.
- HoYoLAB has no public API: the app uses the one its website uses, which HoYoverse can change or block at any time.
- The app contains none of the game’s images. It downloads each one from Project Amber, and the banners’ art from paimon.moe, the first time a page shows it, then keeps it. To use the app offline, Settings → Images downloads them all at once.
